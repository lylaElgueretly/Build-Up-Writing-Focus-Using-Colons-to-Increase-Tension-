Cheat sheet: grammar and its impact on the reader
TechniqueGrammarImpact on the reader
Hint sentenceSimple sentence naming a senseWarns the reader before the character understands.
Very short sentenceSubject and verb, a few wordsMakes the story pause, so the reader holds their breath.
Count sentenceSimple sentence with a numberTells the reader how much is coming, so they wait for it.
ColonPunctuation after a complete sentencePromises detail and makes the reader wait for it.
List with commas and andItems split by commas, and before the lastPiles detail on detail, layer by layer, so there is no escape.
Describing wordsAdjective before a nounMakes every layer specific and unpleasant.
Hyphenated adjectiveWords joined to describe one nounPacks a whole description into one word, so the list feels crowded.
Possessive phraseNoun + ’s shows who it belongs toTies a layer to a real creature or person, so it feels close.
SemicolonSplits a long list into two groupsGives a short breath, then the list starts again.

Step 4: Say how many layers there areCORE

Write a simple sentence that counts the details in your chosen track.
SCAFFOLDThere were ___ layers / signs of ___.

Step 5: Add the colon and list the layersCORE

Add a colon, then list the details from least alarming to most alarming. Separate them with commas and put “and” before the last.
SCAFFOLDThere were ___ layers / signs of ___: a ___, a ___, a ___, a ___, a ___, a ___ and a ___.

Step 6: Add a describing word before each nounCORE

Make each layer stronger with a describing word before the noun. Make the words get stronger as the list goes on.
